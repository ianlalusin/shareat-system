The road to 1 January 2027

Nine phases, sequenced so the riskiest work is proven first. The accounting spine came before everything because every other module posts into it — and because a wrong ledger found in Phase 4 invalidates Phases 2 and 3.

31 December is one calendar entry with three jobs

A valued stock count, a physical asset verification, and the final v1 depreciation run. The asset walk-round is why the capitalisation threshold matters as a number somebody can see and edit — it decides how many things get counted by hand that day. It now sits at ₱5,000, shared by fixed assets and prepaid items, which means more rows to count than ₱10,000 would have — the accepted cost of not absorbing a ₱9,999 purchase into a single month.

If v1’s data were encoded into v2 today

Not a migration plan — v2 always opens on a zero starting point and no history is ever carried across. This asks a different question: if you sat down and re-entered a live v1 record into v2 right now, how much of it would still have somewhere to go? That is the honest test of whether the rebuild has caught up with the thing it replaces.

Method. Every field of v1’s document type was read at source and given exactly one of four verdicts. Field lists were extracted from the code, not remembered; the verdicts are judged.

Carried — same fact, same table Moved — v2 keeps it, in a table that owns it properly Derived — v2 computes it on read, deliberately not stored No home — nowhere in v2 to put it

⚠ A field-name diff would be badly wrong here, and that is the point. Matching v1’s names against v2’s columns scores sales days at 17%. The real figure is 97% — because 55 of those 87 fields did not vanish, they moved to the six tables that should always have owned them. Only the fourth column is a loss.

Attendance 42 fields
98%
★ The most complete module in the system, because the lane that rebuilt it finished. v1’s single AttendanceSessionPlain becomes three tables — attendance_punches (the fact), attendance_adjustments (the decision), attendance_days (the derived) — and that split is why v2 can re-derive a day from truth where v1 cannot.
− shiftId and the two scheduled-time stamps are re-derived from store_attendance_config rather than copied onto the row.
Expenses 46 fields
98%
v1’s expense document carries its own payments, its own VAT split and its own projection state. In v2 those are expense_payments, supplier_invoices and a figure computed on read.
− budgetId — and that one is a decision, not a gap: expense budgets are on the deferred list below because they post nothing to the ledger.
+ journal_entry_id — v1 has no link from an expense to the entry it produced, which is why its books cannot be walked backwards.
Sales days 87 fields
97%
★ The widest document in v1, and the clearest case for the rebuild. Its 87 fields fan out into sales_day_tenders, sales_day_channels, remittances, remittance_variances, statutory_discount_records and — as of today — till_custody_shifts, which finally takes the seven openedAt…closedByUid fields that never belonged on a sales day.
− totalSessions and two denormalised name copies. Nothing anybody reconciles against.
Items 25 fields
76%
Pricing, thresholds and per-store sellability each got their own table, and totalOnHand / costPerUnit are now FIFO figures computed from stock_layers rather than a number somebody can edit.
− barcode, purchaseUnit + conversionFactor, batchTracked, perishable, sortOrder. ⚠ The purchase-unit pair is the one that matters — buying by the sack and issuing by the kilo has no representation in v2 today.
Suppliers 27 fields
67%
⚠ The thinnest master record in v2, and the one a real cutover would notice first. outstandingBalance being derived from the AP ledger is a genuine improvement — v1 stores it and it drifts.
− bankAccounts, vatRegistrationNo, email, contactNo2, representativeName, paymentOptions, issuesOfficialReceipt, lastCreditDueDays, classification. You cannot pay a supplier from v2’s record — the bank details are not in it.
Staff master 38 fields
47%
⚠ Under half — and the reason is a whole phase, not an oversight. v1’s StaffPlain is three records in one: a person, an employment, and a payroll record. v2 has the first two (persons, employments, positions) and none of the third.
− every pay rate (dailyRate, hourlyRate, rate, payType, hourlyRateEffectiveFrom), deductionConfig, all four statutory numbers (sss, philhealth, pagibig, tin), the signature triplet, and the personal-contact block.
★ This is the single largest data gap between the two systems, and it is the honest measure of how much of payroll is still ahead.

What the shape of this actually says

  • The transactional core has caught up. Attendance, expenses and sales days all land at 97–98%: re-entered today, essentially nothing about a day’s trading would have nowhere to go.
  • The master data has not. Suppliers at 67% and staff at 47% are where a real cutover would stall — and neither is a modelling mistake. Both are simply phases that have not been built.
  • ★ Almost every “missing” field is present under a better name. Two-thirds of v2’s apparent losses are fields that moved to a table that owns them, and a further slice are numbers v2 refuses to store because it can compute them — outstandingBalance, totalOnHand, costPerUnit, every remittance state. In v1 each of those is a stored number that can drift from the ledger it claims to summarise.
  • ⚠ Three gaps are worth a decision rather than a shrug: supplier bank details and TIN (you cannot pay from the record), the purchase-unit / conversion-factor pair (you cannot buy by the sack and issue by the kilo), and the whole of payroll.

⚠ These figures describe SHAPE, not readiness. A field having a home in v2 says the schema can hold the fact — not that a screen exists to enter it, nor that anyone has driven it against real data. Read this beside the phase status above, never instead of it.
